Research prototype. This finds pairs of names in your stock list that a string screen scores as similar. It does not say how dangerous any pair is, and it has never been used in a clinical setting or approved by any institution. Every pair it returns needs a pharmacist to look at it. Look-alike names in your own formulary

Upload your stock list. The screen runs in this page, on your computer: nothing is uploaded, stored or transmitted, and the page makes no network connection of any kind.

For a hospital pharmacy, a formulary committee or a medication-safety group. Not for patients, and not a substitute for clinical judgement.

1. Your stock list

A CSV, TSV, Excel workbook or plain text file with one column of product names. Everything else is optional and used if present: ingredient, strength, dosage form, route, manufacturer, storage location, ATC code.

Nothing leaves this page. The file is read and screened by this document in your browser. It is not sent anywhere, and this page has no server to send it to. A formulary is commercially sensitive and that is the reason this tool works the way it does.

This tool ships no drug dictionary. It knows only what your file says. Where your file gives no ingredient and no ATC code, no ingredient or ATC code is inferred and the facts that depend on them are simply absent — they are not guessed. Dosage form and route are read from the words in your own product names where you have not given a column for them, and anything read that way is marked inferred.

How much to show

What this tool does, and what it does not

It compares every pair of names in the list you gave it on five string metrics, and reports the pairs that score above a fixed floor. That is the whole of it. It produces candidates for expert review, not risk assessments, and the reason is not caution for its own sake.

Why there is no verdict here

So there is no validated basis for saying how dangerous a pair is, and the failure would not be symmetric. A pair wrongly marked as nothing to worry about tells a pharmacist to stop worrying about something they should worry about. A pair with no verdict on it at all tells them only to look, which is what this tool is for.

Tall Man lettering

Published Tall Man lists capitalise the segment a human judged to distinguish the two names. A character rule capitalises the segment that differs. Those are not the same judgement, and the gap between them is what the agreement figure measures. Where ISMP publishes a rendering for a pair, this page shows ISMP's and says so. Where it does not, the computed rendering is labelled as computed.

What the ATC facts mean

Different body systems compares the first character of the two ATC codes you supplied. It answers "are these different drugs", not "do they do different things" — prazosin and terazosin are both alpha-1 blockers and diverge only by indication.

What a string metric cannot reach

Facts this tool cannot state

FORMULARY_TOOL.md specifies a fact chip reading "on ISMP's high-alert list". There is no high-alert list in this project, and writing one from memory would be inventing reference data. That chip is therefore absent rather than approximated. Ingredient and ATC inference is absent for a different reason: it needs a reference dictionary, and the licence covering the dataset this project was built from is unresolved, so nothing derived from it is shipped here.
